ADULT PFT RULES AND REPOSITORY INTEGRATION SPECIFICATION
Version 1.0.0 | Reviewed 2026-10-08

1. PURPOSE AND EVIDENCE BOUNDARIES

The application should turn an observed or calculated finding into a selectable report phrase.
It should distinguish three operations: reading a measured value, determining a physiologic finding,
and offering a clinical interpretation. Clicking a number does not establish a diagnosis.

The phrase catalog is original writing. It provides several useful ways to express each clinically
distinct finding; it cannot exhaust all possible synonyms or every clinical context. Some expanded
phrases explain limitations and will be too long for a routine report. Concise is the recommended
default; standard and numeric are usually the most useful alternatives for final laboratory reports.

Source keys refer to the primary standards listed in pft_phrases.json. Official definitions and
thresholds are evidence anchors. Choice of IDs, UI behavior, continuous boundary handling,
near-LLN flags, report-assembly priorities, and wording are proposed implementation conventions.

2. DATA MODEL: STORE THE MEASUREMENTS BEFORE THE WORDING

For each metric, retain:
- measured numeric value and units;
- predicted value, percent predicted, LLN, ULN, and z-score when available;
- reference equation name/version and whether it applies to this person/method;
- test date, posture, method/device, pre/post-bronchodilator state, and medication timing;
- acceptability, repeatability, laboratory quality grade, usability, and reviewer-confirmed reliability;
- source of the value and any correction/adjustment metadata.

The fields below must remain distinct:
- FEV1/FVC measured ratio: 0.62, often displayed as 62%.
- FEV1/FVC percent predicted: a different number; never use it as the measured ratio.
- Change as percent of predicted: (post − pre)/predicted × 100.
- Relative change from baseline: (post − pre)/pre × 100.
- Absolute difference in percent-predicted values: current % predicted − prior % predicted,
  reported as percentage points.

Use a canonical ratio fraction internally and explicit display conversions. RV/TLC, FRC/TLC,
and VA/TLC are also fractions internally. Never apply a percent-predicted threshold to a raw ratio.
Use liters for canonical spirometric volumes and mL for displayed small volume changes.
Use one explicit DLCO unit system per computation. Do not mix mL/min/mmHg with mmol/min/kPa.
Use PD20 units explicitly; micrograms and micromoles are not interchangeable numbers.
Keep FeNO in ppb and its exhalation flow in mL/s. Preserve oxygen device and setting as structured
fields; pulse-dose settings do not represent a universal number of L/min.

For MIP and SNIP, retain signed pressure if supplied and use the magnitude for strength comparisons.
For example, −60 cm H2O is weaker than −100 cm H2O. The algebraically smaller number is not the
weaker effort. MEP is ordinarily displayed as a positive pressure. Use method-appropriate magnitude
references; never compare a signed negative MIP directly with a positive LLN.

Null means unknown. Missing, not measured, invalid, usable with limitations, and reliable are
different states. Never convert unknown to zero, normal, absent, or a negative test.
Do not calculate z-scores from percent predicted using an assumed standard deviation. Use the
validated reference implementation or the laboratory-supplied z-score. Reference equations can be
nonlinear and include transformations. Record domain-specific references rather than assuming
one equation covers spirometry, lung volumes, DLCO, and pressures.

For spirometry, the 2023 ATS statement supports race-neutral references. Apply a valid implemented
reference and record it. Do not retrofit a race-neutral conversion by multiplying historical values
by an improvised factor. Reference changes can affect classification and normalized serial comparisons.

3. QUALITY COMES BEFORE PHYSIOLOGIC CLASSIFICATION

Use documented maneuver quality, not apparent plausibility of the result, to establish reliability.
FEV1 and FVC can have different quality. A maneuver unsuitable for FVC may still yield a usable
FEV1; a cough in the first second can invalidate FEV1 while leaving another component usable.
Low repeatability is a limitation, not automatic proof that all available data are useless.
An inability to meet ideal criteria should be reported with component-specific uncertainty.

Spirometry, lung volumes, DLCO, pressures, challenge measurements, and oximetry each require their
own quality assessment. Do not propagate one global “good quality” flag to every domain.
The 2019 spirometry standard grades FEV1 and FVC separately. For individuals older than six years,
the usual repeatability target is a difference of no more than 150 mL between the two largest
acceptable FEV1 values and separately the two largest acceptable FVC values. This package does
not reproduce the entire acceptability/grading algorithm. Import validated laboratory grades or
implement that algorithm directly from the standard.

For DLCO, repeated high-quality measurements are preferred. Standardized reporting describes at
least two grade-A measurements repeatable within 2 mL/min/mmHg (0.67 mmol/min/kPa). A single usable
effort must be identified as such. These within-session requirements are not thresholds for
longitudinal clinical change. Apply the full current DLCO criteria if computing grades; do not
reduce the algorithm to a single inspired-volume cutoff.

If a component is invalid, suppress definitive pattern/severity language driven by that component.
If values are usable but limited, allow an explicitly reviewed descriptive phrase and attach the
relevant limitation. If a low FVC may result from early termination, do not infer a reliably preserved
ratio or restriction until that concern is resolved.

4. LLN, ULN, BORDERLINE VALUES, AND SEVERITY

Core statistical reference convention: LLN is the fifth percentile (approximately z = −1.645),
and ULN is the 95th percentile (approximately z = +1.645) for an appropriate reference distribution.
Use the actual provided LLN/ULN or validated z-score. Compare unrounded numbers.

Proposed continuous implementation of the 2022 three-level low-value grading:
- not reduced: z ≥ −1.645;
- mild reduction: −2.5 ≤ z < −1.645;
- moderate reduction: −4.0 ≤ z < −2.5;
- severe reduction: z < −4.0.

The published categories are presented with rounded values. The continuous intervals above are a
documented implementation convention to eliminate rounding gaps; do not create an unclassified
interval between −4.0 and −4.1 or between −2.50 and −2.51. Exact equality to the provided LLN is
not “below LLN.” If a laboratory uses a different explicit boundary policy, retain that policy.

Apply this low-value impairment grading to FEV1, FVC, and DLCO with valid references. It grades the
measurement and physiology, not the overall severity of COPD, asthma, ILD, or symptoms. Do not
grade obstruction from FEV1/FVC z-score magnitude. For obstruction, use FEV1 impairment; if the
ratio is low but FEV1 is preserved, say “obstruction with preserved FEV1.” For restriction, the
default should say “restriction with a moderate reduction in FVC,” or state the TLC directly,
rather than silently presenting an unvalidated TLC severity scheme as ATS/ERS grading.

Do not apply these low-value categories to RV/TLC elevations, TLC increases, MIP/MEP deficits,
FeNO, Raw, or oscillometry. Use a separately declared scheme if a specialist laboratory grades them.
Do not define normality with a universal 80% predicted rule. Percent-predicted severity profiles
and GOLD COPD grades can be supported only as named, separate options with their prerequisites;
they are not interchangeable with the default z-score framework.

For historical wording, the 2005 interpretation framework used five FEV1 percent-predicted labels:
mild (>70), moderate (60–69), moderately severe/moderate-to-severe (50–59), severe (35–49), and very
severe (<35). Its printed integer bands require an explicit local convention for unrounded values
and shared/boundary cases. A historically mild label still requires actual abnormality rather than
grading a physiologically normal test merely because percent predicted falls in a band. The
historical DLCO scheme used mild reduction above 60% predicted but below LLN, moderate 40–60%, and
severe below 40%. These are included for interpreting existing lab reports and offering named
legacy wording, not as the default calculations. The legacy-only “moderately severe” and “very
severe” phrases require that their declared FEV1 profile was deliberately selected.

“Near LLN” or “borderline low” is a review flag, not a new official category. The app can make a
near-limit band configurable, but must label it as a local convention and retain the exact
low/normal/high classification. Avoid a border flag that changes a value below LLN to normal.

5. SPIROMETRIC PATTERN RULES

Required: a reliable measured FEV1/FVC with its valid LLN, plus reliable FEV1 and FVC for component
statements. A ratio can be within or above the reference range and still be “preserved” for the
purpose of excluding measured airflow obstruction.

- Obstruction: reliable FEV1/FVC < LLN.
- Normal spirometry: FEV1, FVC, and FEV1/FVC are within their reference ranges, with adequate quality.
- Low FVC with preserved ratio, TLC absent: possible restrictive pattern; restriction unconfirmed.
- Nonspecific pattern: reduced FEV1 and/or FVC, preserved ratio, and TLC within reference limits.
- Low FEV1 alone: report reduced FEV1; do not infer obstruction without the ratio.
- Low ratio with preserved FEV1: obstructive impairment with preserved FEV1; do not infer reduced FEV1.
- High ratio alone: describe the measurement; it does not establish restriction.
- Low ratio plus low FVC, TLC absent: obstruction with reduced FVC; a restrictive component unconfirmed.
- Low ratio plus low FVC, reliable TLC at/above LLN: obstruction without demonstrated restriction.
- Low ratio plus reliable TLC below LLN: mixed obstructive and restrictive impairment.

PRISm is a named phenotype. The frequently used epidemiologic definition uses a fixed preserved
ratio and FEV1 <80% predicted, but definitions differ. A LLN-based descriptive preserved-ratio
finding is not silently equivalent to that definition. Enable the PRISm sentence only when its
declared profile is met. Potential dysanapsis is a reviewed inference, not automatic dismissal of
a low ratio. FEV1/SVC and FEV1/FEV6 require their own validated ratio references and cannot reuse
FEV1/FVC thresholds without justification.

FEF25–75, FEF50/75, or isolated peak-flow deficits should not independently generate a diagnosis
of small airway disease. Changes in FEF25–75 after bronchodilator are not the accepted FEV1/FVC
volume-response test. SVC exceeding FVC may support airway closure after quality and consistency
review, but no disease-defining SVC–FVC difference is hard-coded here.

6. BRONCHODILATOR RESPONSE

Current technical profile, source I22:
  FEV1_response_pct_pred = 100 × (post FEV1 − pre FEV1) / predicted FEV1
  FVC_response_pct_pred  = 100 × (post FVC − pre FVC) / predicted FVC
  A positive increase in either measure greater than 10% of its predicted value qualifies.
  Exactly 10.0% does not satisfy the strictly greater-than criterion.

Use one appropriate predicted value per metric and a valid matched pre/post comparison. Do not
use relative baseline change as the denominator for this criterion. An increase from 60% to
72% predicted is 12 percentage points predicted, whereas 12% relative improvement from a
baseline of 60% predicted is only 7.2 percentage points predicted.

Classify both positive when both metrics qualify; FEV1 or FVC response when that metric qualifies.
If one metric qualifies and the other is unavailable, a positive response is still supported by
the valid metric, but do not say “only FEV1” or “only FVC.” A negative response needs adequate
evaluations of both relevant metrics; incomplete data cannot establish that neither responded.
Numerical dual-metric wording requires all of its listed values. A significant FVC response
alone is a valid response. Its suggested airway-closure mechanism requires review.

Legacy profile: maintain it as a labeled configuration. The historical interpretation standard
describes a ≥12% baseline increase with an absolute increase greater than 200 mL; many subsequent
clinical implementations use ≥200 mL. Store the exact operator and label, rather than silently
collapsing the two boundary policies. Do not combine legacy and current criteria into one test.
Disease-specific diagnostic guidance may use another criterion and belongs in a separate profile.

Post-bronchodilator ratio normalization is independent of the volume-response definition:
- pre ratio < LLN and post ratio ≥ LLN: obstruction is no longer demonstrated after bronchodilator;
- pre and post ratios < LLN: obstruction persists, even if FEV1/FVC volume response is significant.

No measured response does not exclude asthma or medication benefit. A positive response does not
independently establish asthma. A decrease after bronchodilator should prompt review of technique,
fatigue, and timing before “paradoxical bronchospasm” is considered. A recent bronchodilator dose
can alter the result and must be documented when relevant.

7. LUNG VOLUMES

Reliable TLC < LLN confirms restriction. TLC ≥ LLN does not demonstrate restriction in that study.
Never substitute FVC, VA, single-breath TLC estimates, or CT impression for the required reliable
TLC classification. If the method may underestimate total volume or quality is poor, qualify the
finding rather than automatically excluding or confirming restriction.

With restriction, consider the volume fractions and ratio:
- ratio preserved and RV/TLC and FRC/TLC not elevated: simple restrictive pattern is supported;
- ratio preserved and elevated volume fractions/disproportionate retained RV: complex restriction
  can be supported; inspect which fraction is abnormal and select wording that actually fits it;
- ratio below LLN and TLC below LLN: mixed impairment, whether fractions are elevated or normal.

For the specific catalog phrase “complex restriction,” require the increased residual-volume
fraction it mentions. A case with only high FRC/TLC needs reviewed customized wording rather than
a sentence falsely asserting high RV/TLC. Complex restriction can have multiple contributors,
including reduced thoracic expansion or airway closure; no cause is assigned automatically.

With non-reduced TLC and adequate quality:
- high RV/TLC, with FRC/TLC not elevated: an air-trapping volume pattern is supported;
- high FRC/TLC and RV/TLC: hyperinflation is supported, even when TLC is within reference limits;
- high TLC plus elevated fractions: hyperinflation with increased TLC is supported;
- high TLC with preserved fractions: large lungs is a useful descriptive option.

High absolute RV or FRC can support a descriptive abnormal-volume statement when fractions are
missing, but the app should not claim that an unmeasured fraction is normal or high. High RV/TLC
can arise when TLC falls more than RV, or with weakness/limited emptying. With a low TLC, default
to “elevated RV/TLC in restriction,” not automatic air trapping. This conservative default does
not mean air trapping cannot coexist with restriction; it means the mechanism needs review.

Low ERV can relate to body habitus or posture but is not specific for obesity. Low IC can reflect
an elevated resting volume, restriction, or incomplete inspiration; it is not proof of dynamic
hyperinflation. Static PFTs cannot establish exertional dynamic hyperinflation without an
appropriate change-in-IC or other exercise assessment.

Preserve plethysmography, helium dilution, nitrogen washout, and other method metadata. Dilution
may omit poorly communicating gas; plethysmography also has technical limitations. A method
discrepancy is not an automatic quantification of trapped gas or an indication that one result
is always correct. Interpret with test quality and the clinical question.

8. DLCO, HEMOGLOBIN, VA, AND KCO

Classify reliable DLCO using its own valid reference and adjustment basis. Store measured DLCO,
reference-normalized values, the hemoglobin value/date, the method, and exactly what was adjusted.
Standards may adjust the reference comparison rather than simply multiply the measured value.
“Hemoglobin-adjusted DLCO” is useful wording if this accurately describes the lab output. Do not
apply a second correction to already adjusted results.

- A low unadjusted DLCO with documented anemia can partly reflect hemoglobin availability.
- A low hemoglobin-adjusted DLCO remains a gas-transfer deficit on that comparison.
- If adjustment status is unknown, state it is unspecified; do not invent normal hemoglobin.
- Recent smoking, COHb, oxygen conditions, incomplete inspiration, and maneuver quality can
  affect the measurement; assert them only when documented.
- A high DLCO is not proof of asthma, obesity, polycythemia, or hemorrhage.

VA is the sampled single-breath alveolar volume. It is not interchangeable with measured TLC.
KCO is the transfer coefficient, numerically related to DLCO/VA in consistent units; its
reference behavior changes with volume. Do not calculate its predicted value or percent predicted
by dividing the separate DLCO and VA percent-predicted values unless the validated reference
method explicitly defines that calculation.

Permissible component patterns:
- low DLCO + preserved VA + low KCO: lower measured transfer efficiency with preserved sampled volume;
- low DLCO + low VA + low KCO: lower sampled volume and transfer coefficient both contribute;
- low DLCO + low VA + preserved KCO: total transfer remains low despite a preserved coefficient;
- low DLCO + low VA + high KCO: smaller sampled volume with increased coefficient; cause unconfirmed.

Never say a low DLCO “corrects to normal for VA” merely because KCO is normal. Preserve the low
DLCO finding. A normal KCO does not rule out ILD. Low VA does not by itself prove restriction or
extrapulmonary disease. VA/TLC requires an independently measured reliable TLC in comparable
conditions; use an appropriate laboratory reference or reviewed comparison. No universal numeric
VA/TLC cutoff is hard-coded here. A low ratio can indicate incomplete sampling or ventilation
maldistribution, but poor inspiration and method differences must also be considered.

An isolated low DLCO requires preserved valid spirometry and lung volumes; “isolated” cannot be
claimed if either domain is missing. Etiologic suggestions such as pulmonary vascular disease,
emphysema, early parenchymal disease, or anemia require contextual review.

9. RESPIRATORY MUSCLE TESTING

Low pressure magnitude relative to an appropriate reference is a descriptive finding. It can
reflect weakness, effort, mouth seal, technique, posture, or lung-volume mechanics. A normal
measurement is reassuring for that tested function but does not exclude all intermittent or
specific muscle dysfunction. MIP is not an anatomic diaphragm test and MEP is not a complete cough
assessment. In obstructive disease, lung-volume mechanics can affect inspiratory pressures.

Use MIP/MEP/SNIP references appropriate to the method. Do not automatically label them mildly,
moderately, or severely reduced by borrowing FEV1 or DLCO cutoffs. Low MIP plus preserved MEP,
low MEP plus preserved MIP, and low values in both are useful reviewed pattern options. A low
pressure plus restriction can suggest a muscle contribution if both domains are reliable.

Postural VC decrease:
  decrease (%) = 100 × (upright VC − supine VC) / upright VC
Use the same type of VC measurement and reliable efforts. A greater-than-expected fall can
support diaphragmatic dysfunction but does not prove it. The 2019 statement discusses a 15%
fall as a screening clue; baseline mechanics and the clinical question alter its interpretation.
Report the measured percentage and make the concerning/not-excessive clinical comment review-only.
A negative change is an increase when supine; do not print it as a “decrease.”

Peak cough flow needs method and assisted/unassisted status. Disease-specific values commonly
used for airway-clearance reserve are not universal healthy-adult LLNs. Do not automatically
prescribe cough assistance, oxygen, or NIV from a pressure or cough-flow click. ICU NIF thresholds
for ventilator decisions should not be silently repurposed as outpatient MIP reference limits.

10. METHACHOLINE AND INDIRECT CHALLENGE

Methacholine response uses a valid protocol and serial FEV1 measurements, usually with a
post-diluent baseline. Record PD20 when the delivered dose is known and PC20 only with the
documented concentration protocol. Percent fall uses the protocol baseline as the denominator:
  fall (%) = 100 × (baseline FEV1 − dose FEV1) / baseline FEV1
Reached, not reached at maximum dose, incomplete, and technically indeterminate are different states.
If not reached, report PD20/PC20 greater than the completed maximum; do not invent an exact value.

The 2017 table gives these protocol-dependent AHR ranges:
- normal: PD20 >400 micrograms; PC20 >16 mg/mL;
- borderline: PD20 100–400 micrograms; PC20 4–16 mg/mL;
- mild: PD20 25–100 micrograms; PC20 1–4 mg/mL;
- moderate: PD20 6–25 micrograms; PC20 0.25–1 mg/mL;
- marked: PD20 <6 micrograms; PC20 <0.25 mg/mL.
PC20 categories specifically apply to the documented English Wright two-minute tidal protocol;
PD20 requires calculated delivered dose. The printed adjoining ranges share endpoints. Use the
laboratory category or explicitly choose a non-overlapping endpoint policy after validation;
this package deliberately does not auto-assign a category at a shared printed endpoint.
Do not use these categories to grade clinical asthma severity. A borderline AHR category and
a laboratory binary positive result can coexist under the selected definition; avoid contradictory
wording by stating the exact result and criterion. An incomplete study is not a completed negative.

A positive challenge demonstrates hyperresponsiveness, not an independent asthma diagnosis.
A completed appropriate negative test can lower the likelihood of current asthma; dose delivery,
withholding of relevant medication, symptom timing, and the specific phenotype matter. A diluent
response or unstable baseline can make interpretation unreliable. Document recovery with the
actual post-challenge value when it is available.

For exercise/EVH, import the protocol-specific laboratory criterion and document stimulus adequacy,
timing, and serial acceptable FEV1 measurements. The adult ATS exercise framework uses a fall in
FEV1 of at least 10% as the basic diagnostic response, with additional protocol and severity
considerations. Do not label symptoms alone as exercise-induced bronchoconstriction.
Mannitol commonly uses a fall of at least 15% from baseline or 10% between consecutive doses;
verify the adopted protocol. PD15 is a mannitol measure and must not be mislabeled PD20.

11. OPTIONAL ADJUNCT MODULES

FeNO (adult ATS F11 categories, appropriate technique):
- low: <25 ppb;
- intermediate: 25–50 ppb, including the endpoints;
- high: >50 ppb.
Record exhalation flow and test quality. These are clinical interpretation categories, not generic
population LLN/ULN z-score categories. FeNO supports assessment of eosinophilic airway inflammation;
treatment, smoking, atopy, and exposure influence it. Do not diagnose asthma or nonadherence from
FeNO alone. Children require a separate age-appropriate module.

Raw/sGaw: use method-specific references and recorded lung-volume conditions. Describe increased
resistance or reduced conductance; do not use them as independent diagnostic criteria.

Oscillometry: preserve the device, frequencies, reference equation, units, artifact review, and
valid acquisition quality. R5, R5–R20, X5, AX, and resonant frequency have different meanings and
reference limits. A low X5 means more negative reactance, not a simple positive pressure deficit.
R5–R20 is not a direct anatomic peripheral-resistance measurement. No universal device-independent
abnormality or bronchodilator cutoffs are hard-coded here. Import the validated laboratory profile.

Multiple-breath washout: only basic LCI wording is provided. Require tracer/device, quality,
validated reference, and the specialist method standard before implementing calculations.
No universal LCI threshold or Scond/Sacin interpretation algorithm is included.

Walk test: record completed time/distance, oxygen device and setting, aid, stops, symptoms,
continuous SpO2 trace/nadir, and signal quality. A low distance requires a valid reference if stated
as below normal; reduced distance is nonspecific. Exertional desaturation is descriptive and must
not automatically create a diagnosis or oxygen prescription. If stopped before six minutes,
report achieved time and distance rather than a completed six-minute result. “No desaturation”
on supplemental oxygen does not demonstrate normal room-air oxygenation. Resting or exertional
oxygen thresholds should be chosen for the clinical purpose; this package supplies no universal
qualification/treatment cutoff. Full CPET interpretation is outside this handoff.

12. SERIAL COMPARISON AND DISEASE-SPECIFIC CONTEXT

Compare reliable measurements in matched conditions. Use the observed measured change first;
separately state percentage-point predicted change and relative baseline change when useful.
For example, 80% to 74% predicted is a decrease of six percentage points and a 7.5% relative
decrease in the percent-predicted number. Age-related predicted changes mean a relative change
in measured liters may differ from a relative change in percent predicted; identify the denominator.

Do not label every direction of change “significant.” The within-session 150 mL spirometry or
2 mL/min/mmHg DLCO repeatability targets are not universal serial progression criteria. The
bronchodilator >10% predicted threshold is also not a generic longitudinal cutoff. Use a validated
local longitudinal framework or a clinician-reviewed statement of importance.

Check prior/current bronchodilator states, posture, methods, reference equations, hemoglobin
adjustment, and test quality. If these differ, state the comparison limit. A difference in percent
predicted due to a new equation should not be presented as a measured physiologic decline.
An exact or near-exact change can be reported descriptively; “stable/no meaningful change”
requires reviewed comparable data and a declared clinical judgment.

PPF context (P22): in an adult with fibrotic ILD other than IPF, the physiologic progression domain
can be met by an absolute FVC decline of at least five percentage points predicted or an absolute
hemoglobin-adjusted DLCO decline of at least ten percentage points predicted within one year.
PPF requires at least two of the symptom, physiologic, and radiologic domains, with no alternative
explanation. FVC and DLCO changes are both within the same physiologic domain; they do not count
as two distinct PPF domains. Enable the threshold phrases only after the condition, time window,
comparability, and alternative causes are reviewed. An isolated threshold crossing is not PPF.

13. DERIVED FACT CONTRACT FOR pft_phrases.json

The catalog's when predicate is a lookup against the normalized findings object, not raw input.
The repository should implement the preceding rules in a dedicated calculation layer and pass
the findings into a deterministic phrase-selection layer. Conditions use an exact eq comparison.
Unknown facts fail matching. Do not use eval(), implicit truthiness, or free-form LLM inference
to decide a medical threshold. Strings such as “False” are not boolean false.

Required state families:
- spirometry.{fev1,fvc,svc}_state: normal, low, high, not_measured, invalid, or unknown.
- spirometry.{fev1,fvc}_severity: preserved, mild, moderate, severe, or unknown.
- spirometry.ratio_state: normal, low, high, not_measured, invalid, or unknown.
- spirometry.ratio_near_lln: reviewed local flag; fixed_ratio_discordance: fixed_only, lln_only, none, unknown.
- patterns.*: true only when every prerequisite for that pattern is reliable and satisfied.
- patterns.obstruction_severity: preserved, mild, moderate, severe, or unknown, from FEV1.
- bronchodilator.response: fev1, fvc, both, negative, not_performed, indeterminate.
- bronchodilator.current_computable: true only when both metrics' required numeric data are reliable.
- bronchodilator.obstruction_status: normalized, persistent, absent_at_baseline, unknown.
- volumes.{tlc,rv,frc,erv,ic,rv_tlc,frc_tlc}_state: metric-specific state with valid reference.
- volume_patterns.*: compute only from the required reliable absolute volumes/fractions.
- diffusion.{dlco,va,kco}_state: normal, low, high, not_measured, invalid, or unknown.
- diffusion.dlco_severity: preserved, mild, moderate, severe, or unknown.
- diffusion_patterns.*: component combinations with every named state established.
- muscle.{mip,mep,snip}_state: normal, low, not_measured, invalid, or unknown using magnitudes.
- muscle_patterns.*: reliable conjunctions of the named pressure states.
- muscle.supine_drop_computable: same VC type, reliable measurements, positive upright VC, and supine ≤ upright;
  a supine increase requires a separate reviewed sentence rather than the catalog's decrease template.
- challenge.methacholine_state: positive, negative, incomplete, indeterminate, or diluent_response.
- challenge.{pd20,pc20}_reportable and *_exceeds_max: protocol-verified numerical states.
- challenge.{exercise,evh,mannitol}_state: protocol-reviewed positive, negative, or incomplete.
- challenge.exercise_fall_reportable and mannitol_pd15_reportable: validated numerical outputs.
- feno.adult_category: low, intermediate, high, or unknown in an eligible adult measurement.
- integrated.*: conjunctions of the underlying verified pattern/component facts.
- observations.*: only populate from a documented technician/laboratory finding or explicit clinician review.

For compound facts, preserve every qualifier in the actual phrase. Examples:
- patterns.normal_baseline_bd_positive requires normal baseline spirometry and a valid positive response.
- patterns.isolated_low_dlco requires normal reliable spirometry and lung volumes, with low reliable DLCO.
- integrated.all_normal requires the routine test domains actually obtained, reliable, and within reference
  limits, with no contradictory abnormal measured component. If only spirometry was obtained, use
  integrated.spiro_only_normal; the low-DLCO or muscle domains must not be assumed normal.
- volume_patterns.large_lungs requires high TLC and known preserved RV/TLC and FRC/TLC.
- diffusion_patterns.low_dlco_low_va_normal_kco requires low DLCO, low VA, and KCO within reference limits;
  missing KCO is not preserved KCO.

A fact value marks eligibility, not an instruction to insert every matching phrase. Multiple
descriptive options may be eligible for the same underlying finding. Selection and assembly should
choose one useful wording level and suppress redundant statements.

14. METRIC-CLICK UI DESIGN

Map each clicked metric to useful domains:
- FEV1: measured-value options, impairment grade, obstruction pattern only with a valid ratio,
  bronchodilator change if both testing states exist, and comparable serial change.
- FVC/SVC: component wording, low-FVC uncertainty, TLC-supported restriction/nonspecific pattern,
  reviewed SVC–FVC comments, response, and serial change.
- FEV1/FVC: normal/low/high comparison, near-LLN and fixed-ratio discordance review,
  obstruction with preserved/reduced FEV1, and pre/post normalization.
- TLC: confirmed/non-demonstrated restriction, simple/complex/mixed patterns, large lungs,
  high-TLC hyperinflation only with the additional volume evidence, and method/serial limitations.
- RV, RV/TLC, FRC, FRC/TLC: descriptive values, air-trapping/hyperinflation combinations,
  elevated fractions in restriction, and caution about missing denominators or references.
- DLCO: measured value, reference classification, impairment grade, adjustment qualifiers,
  true isolated deficit, VA/KCO patterns, and matched serial comparisons.
- VA/KCO: component value and combined diffusion patterns; never a “corrected to normal” shortcut.
- MIP/MEP/SNIP: magnitude comparison, documented quality limitation, and reviewed pressure patterns.
- upright/supine VC: measured relative postural change and reviewed interpretation.
- challenge: protocol, baseline, maximum delivered dose, reached/not reached/completion status,
  laboratory category, medication limit, and recovery.
- adjuncts: only show their optional modules if that test and a valid profile are present.

Suggested interaction:
1. Click a metric or named pattern.
2. Show supported phrases first, with concise/standard/expanded/numeric choices.
3. Show review-only options in a distinct area with their missing prerequisites or review requirement.
4. Preview the sentence with actual values before adding it to the report.
5. Select a phrase by stable ID and variant; remember that selection, not an arbitrary template string.
6. If an input, quality flag, equation, or profile changes, recompute eligibility and flag invalidated selections.
7. Keep the clinician in control of the assembled report and final wording.

Do not make clinical categories editable only through prose: store the underlying finding, source
profile, variant, inserted values, and review status. A saved report should retain the rule/catalog
version and actual values used, so a later update does not silently rewrite a signed prior report.
Allow ordinary clinician edits, but show that the edited text no longer has guaranteed template consistency.

15. REPORT ASSEMBLY AND SEMANTIC CONFLICTS

Typical report order: quality/limits; spirometry; bronchodilator response; lung volumes; DLCO and
adjustment; additional tests; serial comparison; integrated impression. Omit unperformed optional
tests unless their absence is relevant. Prefer a compact integrated impression with the necessary
component detail rather than inserting every matching metric sentence.

Use the catalog's exclusive_group as one check, not the entire conflict engine. Cross-group rules:
- suppress normal complete PFT/normal spirometry when a relevant reliable abnormality is selected;
- never claim normal complete PFT when only spirometry is available;
- mixed impairment replaces separate primary pure-pattern conclusions, while component values may remain;
- a generic restriction statement can be refined to simple/complex; do not also add “restriction unconfirmed”;
- nonspecific pattern excludes confirmed restriction and requires normal TLC;
- low-TLC/elevated-RV-fraction wording replaces automatic air-trapping wording unless a clinician reviewed coexistence;
- “large lungs with preserved fractions” conflicts with a hyperinflation statement asserting elevated fractions;
- “no significant bronchodilator response” conflicts with a positive response under the same selected profile;
- persistent obstruction may coexist with a significant response; it does not conflict with that response;
- ratio normalization may occur without meeting a volume-response criterion; do not force them to agree;
- a low DLCO cannot be erased by preserved KCO; “normal gas transfer” conflicts with low DLCO on the same basis;
- unadjusted/adjusted/unknown hemoglobin bases must not be simultaneously asserted for the same DLCO comparison;
- a completed negative challenge conflicts with incomplete or indeterminate challenge wording;
- limited reliability cannot coexist with unqualified statements that its invalid component is normal;
- do not attach a normal muscle-strength statement to a valid low MIP or MEP;
- near-LLN review language can accompany low/normal classification; it must not overwrite it;
- a local near-limit label, legacy grade, and z-score grade need their profile labels if shown together.

Specificity priorities for the same finding:
- mixed > simple/complex restriction > generic restriction;
- obstruction with reduced FVC and known TLC > generic obstruction;
- obstruction with preserved FEV1 or a declared FEV1 severity > generic obstruction;
- integrated compound impression > repeated component impression sentences, unless numerical detail is requested.
These are proposed assembly choices. They do not authorize omitting a clinically important abnormal domain.

16. TEMPLATE RENDERING, AUDITABILITY, AND CHECKS

Catalog variants use named placeholders such as {fev1_L}. The required_placeholders map is
variant-specific. Do not insert a numeric template unless every required value and unit is available.
If z-score is absent but the actual value/LLN support classification, choose a nonnumeric phrase
or a clinician-approved simplified numeric variant; do not invent z or print “None.”

Use full-precision values for decisions, then format for display. Displayed rounding must not
change the result: a response slightly above 10% can round to 10.0%, so provide extra precision
or retain an explanatory comparator when needed. Never describe a raw ratio as percent predicted.
Never put source keys, tool filenames, placeholders, hidden fact paths, or implementation instructions
into the copy-ready report. Sources belong in help/reference views or developer documentation.

Verification must cover:
- schema validity, unique IDs, real section/source references, and exact placeholder lists;
- null/unknown behavior, per-metric quality, and unit conversions;
- LLN equality and z-score grading endpoints;
- response at exactly 10%, above 10%, legacy/current discordance, and one missing response metric;
- low FVC with no TLC, normal TLC, and low TLC;
- high RV/TLC with low TLC, high TLC with normal fractions, and hyperinflation with normal TLC;
- low DLCO with normal KCO, unknown hemoglobin status, and missing volume domain;
- signed negative inspiratory pressures and incomplete postural testing;
- PC20/PD20 units, incomplete challenge, and a censored “greater than maximum” response;
- FeNO 25/50 ppb boundaries, age scope, and measurement quality;
- reference/method/correction changes in serial comparisons;
- semantic conflicts and reevaluation after a metric or quality flag is edited.

Fixtures in pft_test_vectors.json specify representative expectations. Integrate them with the
repository's real calculation layer and renderer; do not merely snapshot the JSON catalog.
Any unresolved clinical profile, device reference, boundary policy, or quality algorithm should
remain explicit and disable the corresponding automatic inference until implemented and reviewed.

17. EXAMPLE COMPACT ASSEMBLED REPORTS

A. Normal measured routine domains:
“Acceptable, repeatable spirometry. Spirometry, lung volumes, and DLCO are within reference limits.”

B. Obstruction, retained residual volume, low gas transfer:
“Moderate obstructive ventilatory impairment. No significant bronchodilator response. Lung volumes
show air trapping without restriction. Hemoglobin-adjusted DLCO is moderately reduced.”

C. Low FVC with absent volumes:
“FVC is reduced with a preserved FEV1/FVC. Restriction is unconfirmed without lung-volume measurement.
DLCO is within reference limits.”

D. Low FVC with preserved TLC:
“Reduced FVC with preserved FEV1/FVC and normal TLC, consistent with a nonspecific ventilatory pattern.
DLCO is within reference limits.”

E. Confirmed restriction with a measured impairment grade:
“Restrictive ventilatory impairment with a moderate reduction in FVC. DLCO is moderately reduced.”

F. Response with persistent obstruction:
“Obstructive ventilatory impairment with a significant FEV1 bronchodilator response. Obstruction
persists after bronchodilator. Lung volumes show hyperinflation.”

G. Low total transfer with preserved transfer coefficient:
“DLCO and VA are reduced, with KCO within reference limits. Preserved KCO does not negate the
reduction in total gas transfer.”

H. Low volitional pressures:
“MIP and MEP magnitudes are below their reference limits. This raises concern for respiratory muscle
weakness, although the measurements are effort dependent and technique may contribute.”

I. Incomplete challenge:
“Methacholine challenge was stopped before the planned maximum dose. A fully negative study cannot
be established.”

J. Serial change with explicit basis:
“Compared with the prior study, FVC decreases from 80% to 74% predicted, a decrease of six percentage
points. Hemoglobin-adjusted DLCO is unchanged. The clinical importance of the FVC change requires
review of comparability and the clinical setting.”

All examples assume the named measurements, quality, methods, references, and review findings are
actually available. They illustrate assembly and do not supply findings for any individual patient.
